\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Data and protocol.} We use rainforest soundscapes from multiple RFCx
sites with chainsaw and background labels. Every experiment is
\emph{site-held-out}: all clips from a target site are reserved for test; the
background density is fit on training-site background, prototypes on
training-site positives, and the decision threshold is calibrated on a
training-site background validation split to background FP $\le 0.10$. This
mirrors the closed-set calibration exactly, so the two are directly comparable.
We report the primary held-out site (tambopata) and $N$ additional
leave-one-site-out folds.

\paragraph{Metrics.} \emph{Flagged recall} (positives with $s \ge \tau$),
\emph{attributed recall} (flagged \emph{and} argmax equals the true class),
\emph{background FP}, and the area under the recall--vs--background-FP curve of
the anomaly score alone (attribution-independent separability).

\subsection{Closed-set classifiers do not travel}

Table~\ref{tab:headline} restates the closed-set baseline: the forest-trained
CNN reaches $0.976$ chainsaw recall at raw argmax on the held-out site, but at
that operating point $50\%$ of background is flagged as a threat. Calibrated to a
deployable background FP, recall collapses to $0.195$. The model contains a real
chainsaw-like signal but no cross-site decision boundary. Re-using that same CNN
as the open-set \emph{embedding} inherits the bias: its anomaly-score AUC is
$0.319$ on the held-out site, below chance.

\subsection{Embedding matters more than machinery}

The open-set machinery is identical across embeddings, isolating the effect of
$\phi$. The forest-CNN embedding inherits the same domain bias that sinks the
closed-set model (score AUC $0.15$--$0.56$ across folds, near chance), whereas
the frozen AudioSet CNN14 lifts score AUC above chance at every held-out site
($0.55$--$0.71$; Table~\ref{tab:folds}). On the primary site it reaches $0.317$
flagged recall at $0.056$ background FP --- exceeding the closed-set calibrated
recall of $0.195$ while requiring \emph{zero} labeled positives for flagging.
This supports our central design claim: with a sufficiently general frozen
embedding, background-only density is a stronger \emph{deployable} flagging
recipe than a supervised closed-set head.

Two honest caveats temper this. First, absolute recall is modest ($0.27$--$0.37$
where the FP budget is met): faint, distant chainsaws under an unseen forest's
chorus remain hard. Second, the threshold calibrated on training-site background
does not always hold the FP budget on an unseen site (warsi $0.20$, romania
$0.13$; Table~\ref{tab:folds}), a cross-site \emph{calibration} gap distinct from
the separability the embedding provides. Both point to the same remedy we cannot
yet apply offline --- a small amount of destination-site background, which every
deployment records on day one --- and motivate the field validation in
Section~\ref{sec:deployment}.

\subsection{Attribution lights up with a handful of labels}

Figure~\ref{fig:kcurve} plots attributed recall against the number of verified
positives per class $k \in \{0,1,5,10,25\}$ (mean $\pm$ std over seeds), with
flagging held fixed. At $k=0$ every anomaly is honestly unknown by construction
($0.000$ attributed recall); on tambopata attribution rises to $0.11$ at $k=5$
and $0.18$ at $k=25$, tracking flagged recall as prototypes sharpen. Flagging is
identical across all $k$ by construction --- the confirmations only move mass
from \emph{unknown} onto the named class, exactly the day-one-useful,
self-improving behavior a ranger-in-the-loop deployment needs.

\subsection{Honest unknowns}

To test abstention we remove chainsaw's prototype entirely and score the
held-out site's chainsaw clips. A well-behaved open-set detector should still
\emph{flag} these clips (they are acoustically unusual) while assigning their
likelihood mass to \emph{unknown} rather than misattributing it. This is exactly
what we observe: on every site with held-out chainsaws, flagging is unchanged
and $100\%$ of flagged clips are predicted \emph{unknown} with mean unknown mass
$1.0$ --- the detector never invents a wrong label for a class it was never
shown.

\subsection{Faintness: SNR sensitivity}

Real threats are often distant and faint. We build a controlled evaluation by
mixing positive events into background at fixed signal-to-noise ratios and
measuring flagged recall per SNR, giving an operating characteristic in the
regime that matters for early detection. The harness is validated on RFCx
background and runs unchanged against destination-site field background on
arrival.
