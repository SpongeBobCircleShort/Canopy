\section{Introduction}

Illegal logging is a leading driver of tropical forest loss, with organized
criminal activity implicated in a large share of it \citep{nellemann2012greencarbon},
and its most reliable early signature is sound: chainsaws and vehicles are
audible under canopy that hides the activity from optical satellites until after
the trees are down. Passive acoustic monitoring has consequently become a
standard conservation instrument, to the point that its bottleneck has shifted
from collecting audio to interpreting it
\citep{stowell2022bioacoustics,malerba2026pam}. The dominant recipe is a closed-set
classifier: train a CNN on labeled clips of $k$ threat classes, deploy, and
alert when a class probability crosses a threshold. This is not a strawman we
construct: it is the recipe fielded conservation-acoustic systems actually run,
and a global expert assessment of the field identifies manual validation of
detector output as a standing bottleneck, since practitioners do not act on raw
detections unchecked \citep{malerba2026pam}. The failure mode
we measure is thus a property of the deployed paradigm, not of a weak baseline.

This recipe embeds two assumptions that fail in conservation deployments.
First, it assumes labeled positives from the deployment domain exist. They
rarely do: a new reserve has no verified recordings of chainsaws \emph{under its
own microphones, weather, and species chorus}, and public datasets are dominated
by close-range, high-SNR urban recordings. Label scarcity is in fact the standing
premise of the bioacoustics literature, which is why few-shot formulations are
the field's dominant response \citep{stowell2022bioacoustics,nolasco2023dcase}. Second, it assumes the threat
taxonomy is known in advance, so any sound outside the $k$ classes is silently
mapped onto them or suppressed.

We first quantify how badly the closed-set recipe travels, and diagnose
\emph{why} (Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis}). Across four held-out rainforest sites,
with leakage-checked splits and validation-calibrated thresholds, we find three
things. \textbf{(1) Random splits are optimistic}: a classifier at $0.907$
in-domain accuracy detects essentially no chainsaws on any held-out site. This is
the acoustic instance of a known hazard in spatially structured ecological data,
where random cross-validation ignores dependence between nearby samples and
underestimates predictive error, and block or grouped splits are the corrective
\citep{roberts2017crossval}.
\textbf{(2) The failure is operating-point transfer, not discrimination}: the
score still ranks chainsaws above background (ROC-AUC up to $0.96$), but the
source-calibrated threshold yields $0$ recall in the target domain, so
false-positive control at a fixed budget, not accuracy, is the binding
constraint. That a model's scores decalibrate under distribution shift even while
its ranking survives is a documented failure mode
\citep{snoek2019shift}; our contribution is to show that it, rather than
discrimination, is what actually blocks deployment here. \textbf{(3) The representation encodes site}: recording site is
linearly decodable from the embedding far above chance, and more strongly than
event class, giving a mechanism for the failure. Sensor- and site-dependent
background structure is known to break bioacoustic detectors moved to a new
recording unit \citep{lostanlen2019robust}; we show it is legible directly in
the representation, before it shows up in any metric. A CNN trained on rainforest
soundscapes with a site held out confirms the pattern, reaching only $0.195$
recall at a deployable false-positive rate despite $0.976$ raw-argmax recall.
Our open-set detector, over a frozen general-purpose embedding, recovers a
deployable operating point on the same held-out site, reaching $0.317$ flagged recall
at $0.056$ background FP, exceeding the closed-set recall while using no labeled
positives to decide what is anomalous.

We argue the deployable formulation is open-set and background-first:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Flag} a clip by how far it deviates from \emph{this forest's}
      normal soundscape, a density fit on unlabeled background audio alone,
      which every deployment can record on day one;
\item \textbf{Attribute} flagged clips against per-class prototypes built from
      whatever ranger-verified positives exist so far, zero at launch;
\item \textbf{Abstain honestly}: when no prototype matches, report
      \emph{unknown} rather than forcing a known label.
\end{enumerate}
The decision threshold is calibrated to an explicit background false-positive
budget, because ranger attention, not accuracy, is the scarce resource.

Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item A diagnosis of closed-set failure under site-level shift with an audited,
      leakage-free, multi-site protocol (Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis}), separating
      three causes with bootstrap-quantified evidence: split optimism, an
      operating-point (not discrimination) failure in which false-positive
      control is the binding constraint, and a representation that encodes
      recording site more strongly than event class. To our knowledge this is the
      first study to attribute bioacoustic threat-detection failure to
      site-encoding via a linear-probe analysis.
\item A background-only open-set detector, built from a shrunk-covariance Mahalanobis
      density over a frozen pretrained embedding, quantile-mapped scores, an
      FP-budget threshold, and prototype attribution with reserved unknown
      mass, that ships with zero labeled threats and improves monotonically
      as verified positives arrive, without retraining.
\item A site-held-out evaluation across four RFCx sites measuring flagging,
      attribution, the attribution-vs-verified-positives curve, and
      honest-unknown behavior under class holdout. The embedding, not the
      detector head, governs cross-site deployability: frozen general-purpose
      encoders clear chance separability at every site where the site-trained
      representation does not.
\item A measurement of what label-free operation costs. A supervised probe on
      the \emph{same} frozen embeddings, under the identical protocol, gives the
      label-rich upper bound per representation (weighted AUROC $0.783$ AudioSet,
      $0.786$ BirdNET, $0.648$ site-trained). Labels roughly double recall, but
      the label-free detector is the one that runs on day one. We also show an
      embedding can rank well and still be undeployable: BirdNET is the best
      probe substrate yet flags nothing under a transferred threshold, which is
      the same operating-point failure in unsupervised form.
\item An open-source deployment in which the ranger review loop
      closes the label flywheel: confirmed alerts become prototype updates.
\end{itemize}
