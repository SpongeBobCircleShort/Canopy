\title{Listening Without Labels: Open-Set Acoustic Threat Detection\\
for Forest Conservation from Background Audio Alone}
\author{Anonymous submission}

\begin{abstract}
Acoustic monitoring is one of the cheapest scalable defenses against illegal
logging, but deployed detectors are typically closed-set classifiers trained on
public or urban recordings, and we show they do not survive contact with a new
forest: a CNN chainsaw detector with strong in-domain validation collapses to
0.195 recall on a fully held-out site once calibrated to a deployable
false-positive rate. We argue the deployment-shaped constraint for conservation
acoustics is not ``classify $k$ known threats,'' but ``flag what is unusual for
\emph{this} forest, attribute it when evidence exists, and say \emph{unknown}
otherwise --- starting from zero labeled threats.'' We present an open-set
detector that fits a background density on unlabeled forest-at-rest audio over a
frozen pretrained embedding, calibrates its decision threshold to a background
false-positive gate, and attributes flagged clips with per-class prototypes
built incrementally from ranger-verified positives, reserving honest
\emph{unknown} mass for sounds matching no prototype. On a held-out rainforest
site, a frozen AudioSet embedding lifts chainsaw-vs-background separability from
chance (the forest-CNN embedding's AUC $0.32$) to AUC $0.71$, and the detector
flags $0.317$ of chainsaws at a $0.056$ background false-positive rate ---
exceeding the calibrated closed-set recall of $0.195$ while using no labeled
positives for flagging. When a class's prototype is withheld, every flagged clip
is honestly labeled \emph{unknown}.
The detector is deployed in Canopy, an open-source forest monitoring platform,
where ranger confirmations close the loop: each verified alert becomes a
prototype update, with no retraining. We release code, fitted artifacts, and
the full evaluation protocol.
\end{abstract}
